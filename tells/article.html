The tell

In poker, a tell is the move a player makes without knowing he made it. It gives away his hand before the cards do.

On chain, a dev makes the same kind of move. He funds a fresh wallet. He splits his bag across five wallets so the holder chart looks honest. He loads a side wallet an hour before he needs it. Each move is small. Each move is visible. Nobody is watching for it, because watching one wallet is easy and watching the whole pattern is work.

TELLS does that work.

What TELLS watches

Three things, on every coin you hold.

The dev, and everyone tied to him. Not just the creator wallet. The whole cluster: who funded him, which fresh wallets he funded, where his exits go. A wallet joins the watch list with evidence, a transaction that ties it to the cluster. When a cluster wallet sells, moves coin to a fresh wallet, or starts loading up, you hear about it.

The top holders, as a living list. The list is re-read from the chain every cycle. A whale climbing in gets watched. A whale heading out stays watched until it is truly gone. Wallets that share one funder count as one holder, so five wallets at just under 1 percent each read as what they are: one holder at 4.5 percent. When new money arrives, the alert says where it came from. Independent money with a real history is one thing. A fresh wallet the dev cluster funded an hour ago is another thing, and the alert says which one it is.

The coin itself, and your own lines. Liquidity pulled. A lock about to expire, flagged before the date, not after. Mint or freeze authority moving. Migration. A pool draining fast. And the lines you set from your own entry: a stop, a first take profit, a second. When price touches your line, your phone buzzes.

Your lines never execute. TELLS holds nothing, signs nothing, trades nothing. It watches and it reports. The call is always yours.

Three kinds of loud

Not every event deserves to wake you. TELLS sorts what it sees into three tiers.

INFO is the quiet note. New money arriving. A move worth knowing about tomorrow. It batches into a daily digest.

ALERT is delivered now. A probable side wallet loading. Your take profit line touched. A lock ending inside a day.

FIRE is the one that has to get through. A dev cluster selling size. Several top holders leaving together. Liquidity pulled. Your stop touched. FIRE is rare on purpose. It only fires on size, on cluster evidence, or on several wallets moving at once. A small wallet doing a small thing is never FIRE.

Where it reaches you

Telegram and Discord are free. Every alert, instantly, forever. Nothing about the warning is paid, and nothing about its speed is paid.

The phone channel is the paid part. A text to your number, and if you want it, a call behind it that rings until you answer or tell it to stop. No app to download. The front door is your phone number. You enter it, you prove it is yours with one code text, and that is the whole setup.

What is sold is the wake-up channel. Never the warning. Never its speed.

The record

Every alert TELLS sends is written to a catch log first, before it is delivered. The log is public at launchkey.fun/tells/log. It shows the date, the coin, the event, and what happened next. Misses get logged too, in the same format. A catch log with no misses in it is a lie, and nobody should trust one.
