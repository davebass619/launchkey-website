Where BONDED Stands

BONDED is live. The first bonded coin launched on November 11, 2026, and its 5 SOL bond is being measured now. The rules measure activity from the developer wallet and a fixed set of wallets linked by a published funding rule. A link under that rule does not prove common ownership. Activity from wallets outside the set is not covered. The proposed design has no challenge or appeal stage. A published execution can be voided only if its measurement fails the stated recomputation test against chain data. Outcomes execute through the program.

How BONDED Works

The Proposed Design, Step by Step

1. Before a coin launches through the door, the developer posts a bond in SOL. The terms for that bond are fixed before it is posted.

2. During the bond window, the program tracks selling and transfers out of the launch position by the developer wallet and a fixed set of wallets linked under a published funding rule. A link under that rule does not prove common ownership. Activity from wallets outside the set is not covered.

3. If a breach is confirmed, the program automatically distributes the forfeited share of the bond and the same share of the creator's fees still held in the vault to the eligible wallets in the execution block snapshot, pro rata. Holders do not need to claim.

4. If no breach is confirmed, the bond returns to the developer.

5. There is no challenge or appeal stage, and nobody at BONDED decides the outcome by hand.

The Standard Bond

5 SOL. A 24 hour window. The same for every dev.

A break never raises it. The next launch posts the same 5 SOL.

A dev can buy the window down: 2 SOL for each hour, down to 12 hours.

Creator fees on a bonded launch sit in the coin's own bond vault. They release to the dev in quarters as the window runs, the rest at close. Released fees are the dev's to keep. Creator fees still held at a confirmed breach pay out to the holders with the bond, under the same forfeited share.

There is no door fee and no bond fee.

Why the Trenches Need It

The Problem BONDED Is Built For

Launching a coin costs a dev nothing. He can sell into his own buyers, walk away, and launch again tomorrow under a fresh wallet. The buyers carry the loss.

A bond changes who pays for failure. The dev puts real money down before the first buy. If he does what he said, he gets it back. If he does not, the bond is what his holders are paid from.

No promises to trust. Money posted up front, rules published in advance, outcomes that execute on their own.
