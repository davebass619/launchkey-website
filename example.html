Attribution — observed vs inferred
OBSERVEDEvery launch above names this wallet as its creator in the on-chain record. The counts are arithmetic on public transactions — anyone can recount them.
INFERREDThat one person or one team controls this wallet. The record attributes launches to the wallet. It does not establish who holds the keys, and the grade never claims it does.
3
Classifications — mechanical labels only
17 prior coins carry the peak-and-collapse ending under the desk's published proxy: ATH ≥ $20K, then a fall below 10% of ATH and under $5K. That is a measurement of how those coins ended — printed as measured, not as a claim about why.
The cadence is also a measurement: 20 launches in 24 hours, on top of 500+ recorded. Volume at that rate is itself record data.
4
Grade
PROBATION · ADVERSE RECORD
The key exists — everyone launches. The grade sits at Probation and the adverse record prints on the file in the open: 500+ launches · 17 peak-and-collapse endings · 20 more in the last 24 hours. The coin's story — a genuine news narrative — changes nothing. The grade reads the wallet, not the pitch.
5
The same for every key
Placement: every launch lists the same way, newest first. Grades do not buy position.
Pairing: the full catalog is open to every key from its first launch
The way back is also mechanical: launch, don't produce exit events, build endings that aren't collapses — the record updates and the grade can climb. Nothing about this grade is permanent except the record underneath it.

Attribution — observed vs inferred
OBSERVEDThe launch, the graduation, and the price path above.
UNREADABLEThe funding trail behind this wallet. The coin's birth sits under ≥42,000 signatures — beyond the trace budget — so where this wallet's money came from could not be read. The method stops there and labels it, instead of guessing.
3
Classifications — mechanical labels only
One GRADUATED ending. No exit event is recorded. No liquidity withdrawal is recorded. No adverse classification applies — and none is invented to fill the gap.
The provenance step returns UNREADABLE, which is a statement about our tooling, not about this dev.
4
Grade
PROVEN
An unreadable trail is not a verdict — and it is not a veto either. No adverse finding is printed, because there is no adverse evidence. And the published rule asks only what the record shows: one launch, one real graduation — a launch that completed its curve without collapsing from its peak — and no adverse marks. That is PROVEN. The trail we could not read costs the key nothing; the graduation it earned carries it.
5
The same for every key
Placement: every launch lists the same way, newest first. Grades do not buy position.
Pairing: the full catalog is open to every key from its first launch
Same two lines as the clear case — they hold for every key, this one included. This key's record is one real graduation and a question the chain hasn't answered yet. What it does next decides whether the grade holds, climbs, or falls — not what we couldn't read.
