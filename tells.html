The tell

In poker, a tell is the move a player makes without knowing he made it. It gives away his hand before the cards do.

On chain, a dev makes the same kind of move. He funds a fresh wallet. He splits his bag across five wallets so the holder chart looks honest. He loads a side wallet an hour before he needs it. Each move is small. Each move is visible. Nobody is watching for it, because watching one wallet is easy and watching the whole pattern is work.

TELLS does that work. It is a read-only guard for the coins you already hold. It holds nothing, signs nothing, and trades nothing.

What TELLS watches
The dev cluster

Not just the creator wallet. The whole cluster: who funded him, which fresh wallets he funded, where his exits go. A wallet joins the watch with evidence, a transaction that ties it in. When a cluster wallet sells, moves coin to a fresh wallet, or starts loading up, you hear about it.

Top holders, a living list

Re-read from the chain every cycle. A whale climbing in gets watched. A whale heading out stays watched until it is truly gone. Wallets that share one funder count as one holder, so five wallets under 1 percent each read as what they are: one holder at 4.5 percent. New money arrives with its provenance attached, independent or cluster-funded, and the alert says which.

The coin, and your lines

Liquidity pulled. A lock about to expire, flagged before the date, not after. Mint or freeze authority moving. Migration. A pool draining fast. And the lines you set from your own entry: a stop, a first take profit, a second. When price touches your line, your phone buzzes.

Your lines never execute. Your call. TELLS does not sell anything. It watches and it reports.

Three kinds of loud
INFO

The quiet note. New money arriving. A move worth knowing about tomorrow. It batches into a daily digest.

ALERT

Delivered now. A probable side wallet loading. Your take profit line touched. A lock ending inside a day.

FIRE

The one that has to get through. A dev cluster selling size. Several top holders leaving together. Liquidity pulled. Your stop touched. FIRE is rare on purpose: it only fires on size, on cluster evidence, or on several wallets moving at once.

The phone channel is the paid part. A text to your number, and if you want it, a call behind it that rings until you answer or tell it to stop. No app to download. The front door is your phone number: you enter it, you prove it is yours with one code text, and that is the whole setup.

What is sold is the wake-up channel. Never the warning. Never its speed.

The record

Every alert TELLS sends is written to a catch log first, before it is delivered. The log is public. It shows the date, the coin, the event, and what happened next. Misses get logged too, in the same format. A catch log with no misses in it is a lie, and nobody should trust one.
