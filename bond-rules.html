The bond rules,
in plain language.

BONDED is live.

The standard bond

One bond. The same for every dev.

5 SOL, posted before the launch.

Held for 24 hours.

No door fee. No bond fee.

What is measured

The creator's position is frozen as a baseline at the coin's birth.

The program measures selling and transfers out of that position.

It counts the creator wallet and wallets linked to it by the published funding rule.

Exits count cumulatively across the whole window. Tokens that leave do not come back on the count.

What counts as a breach

A confirmed exit above the free band below.

Nothing else. A coin going quiet is not a breach. Moves after the window closes are not measured.

What a break costs

Hold your coins for the full 24 hours and the whole bond comes back.

Sell or move up to 20 percent and the whole bond still comes back.

Past that line, on a 5 SOL bond:

Sell 30 percent, lose 0.5 SOL.

Sell 40 percent, lose 1 SOL.

Sell 50 percent, lose 1.5 SOL.

Sell 60 percent, lose 2 SOL.

Sell 75 percent, lose 2.75 SOL.

Sell 90 percent, lose 3.5 SOL.

Sell 100 percent, lose 4 SOL.

Coins that leave, come back, and leave again keep counting, and that is the only way the loss reaches the whole 5 SOL.

The part you lose goes to the holders, and the same share applies to creator fees still held in the vault at settlement.

Creator fees

Creator fees are designated into the coin's own bond vault at birth.

They release to the dev in quarters at the window's quarter points: 25, 50, and 75 percent of what has accrued, and the rest at close.

Fees already released are the dev's to keep. They are never clawed back, even on a breach.

Buy-down

At creation, a dev can buy the window down by posting more bond.

2 SOL of added principal per whole hour, down to a 12 hour floor.

The added principal is bond, not a fee. It counts toward the forfeiture math like the rest.

How to keep your bond

The system reads one thing: how much of the creator position left during the window.

It counts the creator wallet and wallets linked to it by funding.

Nothing else is read. Price does not matter. Volume does not matter. A quiet coin is fine.

The first 20 percent can go free.

Keep exits at or under 20 percent for the full window and the bond comes back in full.

Fees released at the checkpoints are yours. They are never taken back.

These rules are the whole test. They print before you bond, they are the same for everyone, and the program runs them the same way every time. No person decides.

How a payout works

The bond and the creator fees sit in the coin's own vault. It is a program address. No person holds a key to it. Not the dev. Not us.

If the terms are broken, the program takes the same share of the bond and of the fees still in the vault.

That share becomes a pool, and the program splits it between the holders, each wallet paid for its share of the supply it held.

The dev cannot stop it, delay it, or change the amount. There is no step where anyone asks him.

If the terms are kept, the bond goes back to the dev, and the fees release on the checkpoint schedule.

Settlement

Hold the terms for the full window and the bond returns.

A confirmed breach executes automatically at the window's end.

The forfeited share is split pro rata between the eligible holder wallets by the program.

The forfeiture and the permanent mark on the record are the whole consequence. A break never raises the bond. The next launch posts the same 5 SOL.

No vote. No appeal. No claim to file.

These rules are the plain language version. The full terms are the bond terms of record.

BONDED is a door, not a promise. Most coins go to zero. The grade prints where everyone can see it.
