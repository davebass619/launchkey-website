One Question
How is this different from locking a dev buy in Streamflow for 24 hours?
A lock is a timer on the dev's own tokens. A bond is SOL the holders can actually collect.
A lock always pays the dev back at expiry, whatever he did. A bond is forfeit on a confirmed breach, and the SOL splits between holders by the program.
A lock covers one wallet's tokens and says nothing about side wallets or creator fees. The bond measures the creator position against a frozen baseline, and escrows creator fees in the coin's own vault, releasing in quarters at the checkpoints. On a confirmed breach, the same forfeited share of the creator fees still held pays out to the holders with the bond.
A lock is any amount and duration the dev chooses. The bond is one flat standard, 5 SOL and 24 hours, terms printed before the coin exists, and the outcome published either way.
The limit, plainly: both end. BONDED sells one window where breaking your word costs real SOL, not lifetime safety.
What a Grade Is Not
●
Not a promise. A grade is a record, not a claim that a coin is safe or a dev is trustworthy. The methodology prints what the grading rule gets wrong, in its own numbers.
●
PROBATION is not a ban. It is where every new key starts, and it launches from day one.
●
PILLAR is not an endorsement. It is the strongest measured record on the pad. Records change as new launches land.
●
The record has limits, and they are printed. Exits routed through wallets the published criteria do not link are outside the measurement, and the Grading Rules say so.
