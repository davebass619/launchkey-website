How a key gets its grade.
How grades are made, what they mean, and what they never mean. Methodology v2.
For the bond itself, read the bond rules in plain language.
The Principle
Everyone launches. Every key is graded.
We grade hands, not luck — what the dev did, not what the market did.
This is a record, not a promise. Grades summarize observed wallet activity under a published method. They do not establish identity, intent, or whether holders were harmed. A higher grade means the observed record meets that tier’s criteria, not that the record is complete or the next launch is safe.
What We Measure
●
Launch-wallet activity — the launches, buys, sells, liquidity actions, and endings associated with the launch wallet.
●
Attributed wallets — additional wallets attributed to the launch wallet under the published methodology. Observed transactions and inferred wallet relationships are displayed separately.
●
Evidence on every finding — each finding identifies its source transactions, observation period, methodology version, and any wallet-attribution assumptions.
●
Coverage has limits — activity outside the supported protocols, observation period, or identified wallets may not appear in the record. An absence of detected events is not evidence that no event occurred.
●
Conduct on this platform — every launch here adds to the same file while the service operates. Corrections annotate the record; they never overwrite it.
●
Measurements print continuously; labels are mechanical — wherever the desk measured one, a number prints as a number: holdings over time, disposal share, cluster share, counts. A label — an ending, an adverse exit finding, a grade — fires only when a published definition is met. A measurement below a label's threshold is still printed; it simply carries no label.
●
Social links, labeled two ways — a coin's file labels every social link one of two ways: linked by launcher, where the claimed account publicly references the coin or its file, so the link is proven from both sides; or claimed, unlinked, where the link appears in the coin's metadata only. An unlinked claim prints as a claim, never as the project's account.
How a Coin Can End
GRADUATED
Completing the bonding curve and continuing to trade are separate observations. Neither establishes that holders profited or that the developer acted properly. Any effect on the grade follows the published scoring rules.
QUIET DEATH
The coin faded without a measured exit event. This barely touches the grade — a flop is not a crime.
POST-LAUNCH EXIT EVENT
A measured fact: net token disposals by the launch wallet and its attributed wallets reached at least 50% of attributed holdings during the first 60 minutes after launch. The event record shows the holdings baseline, included wallets, attribution basis, calculation, and source transactions. Transfers are not treated as sales solely because tokens moved between wallets. An exit event is a fact on the record, and by itself it carries no grade consequence — selling early is common, and on its own it is not a finding against anyone. It does not establish intent, common control of every attributed wallet, or harm to holders.
SLOW EXIT — PROPOSED
Also a fact: a measured disposal of at least 50% of attributed holdings that completes after the first 60 minutes and within 7 days of launch (the completion window is stated in the methodology annex). A slow exit prints like any other exit. Like a fast one, it moves a grade only through an adverse exit finding.
ADVERSE EXIT FINDING
The only exit that moves a grade. An exit becomes an adverse exit finding only when at least one published aggravator is also measured: deception — the disposal contradicts a lock or hold position printed at launch; extraction + abandonment — cumulative disposal of at least 90% within 7 days, no later launch-wallet market activity on the coin, and a quiet-death or abandoned-with-holders ending; or hidden position — the position printed at birth materially understated the attributed cluster's true position, and the hidden share was disposed of. An aggravator the desk data cannot check is printed as unchecked — never presumed.
ABANDONED — WITH HOLDERS
An ending class: the coin reached a real peak — all-time high at or above $20,000 — and its last read sits below the lower of 10% of that peak or $5,000. The class names the market state. It says nothing about why, and on its own it is not an exit finding.
OBSERVED LIQUIDITY WITHDRAWAL
A separate finding: liquidity was measurably removed from a pool by an attributed wallet during the stated observation period. The finding carries its own evidence and definition; it is not folded into the exit-event calculation.
CORRELATED EXIT PATTERN
A separate finding: disposals by multiple attributed wallets met the methodology’s published correlation test. Correlation and timing alone do not establish common control.
OBSERVED INACTIVITY — PROPOSED
A defined period with no observed activity from the launch wallet or its attributed wallets while those wallets still hold tokens from the launch. Inactivity alone does not establish abandonment, disappearance, or a broken obligation.
How a record becomes adverse. There are three measured paths, and no others in the published rule. Any one of them prints the key as PROBATION · ADVERSE RECORD.
1. An adverse exit finding. A measured exit event that also carries at least one published aggravator above — deception, extraction + abandonment, or hidden position.
2. The peak-collapse pattern. Three or more of the key’s launches meet the peak-collapse test: an all-time high at or above $20,000, and a last read below the lower of 10% of that peak or $5,000.
3. The launch-farm pattern. The key has 100 or more launches, its graduation rate is under 2%, and it made five or more launches in the last 24 hours.
The second and third paths are key-level grade triggers. They are not coin endings and they are not exit findings; they read the key’s whole record. How findings affect grades. A finding affects a grade only under the published scoring rules. Where the evidence is insufficient to apply a rule, the result is marked UNDETERMINED.
The Tiers — And What They Unlock
PROBATION
Where every new or unknown key starts.
Probation means insufficient history. It is not a finding of misconduct.
A key with measured adverse findings sits here too — printed as PROBATION · ADVERSE RECORD, a different label from plain Probation.
PROVEN
No adverse record, and proof on it: one real graduation — a launch that completed its curve without collapsing from its peak — or, across two or more launches, at least one completed curve or one launch still trading.
PILLAR
The deepest record on the pad: three or more launches, at least one graduation, at least one launch that reached a $100K all-time high, and no adverse record — over a record at least 30 days old.
Placement: every launch lists the same way, newest first. Grades do not buy position.
Pairing: the full catalog is open to every key from its first launch.
Under the published scoring rules, one genuine success outweighs many quiet flops. Ten coins that faded cost a key almost nothing. One coin that landed can carry it.
And an adverse record drops a key on the spot — whether it comes from an adverse exit finding or one of the two key-level patterns in §3, whatever tier it was sitting in. A plain exit event drops nothing; it prints on the record either way. Grades climb slowly. They fall fast.

Funding Links
A funding link shows a transaction path between wallets. It does not, by itself, establish shared ownership, control, intent, or responsibility for another wallet’s activity.
Where a funding path is reliably attributed under the published methodology and the source wallet has measured exit events, the new key can start at the bottom tier. Where provenance affects the starting tier, the record identifies the relevant transfers, the rule applied, and the uncertainty involved. The restriction is a provenance-based starting position, not inherited misconduct.
Funding paths that cannot be reliably attributed — including paths through shared services or exchanges — are marked UNRESOLVED. An unresolved path is not an adverse finding.
A wallet’s own observed activity is assessed separately from its funding links.
We call that starting position an inherited shadow. It is a starting position, not a finding about the new key.
Corrections and Review
A record can be disputed for its transaction classification, wallet attribution, calculation, or rule application. Message us on X at @postthebond and provide supporting evidence where available.
A dispute does not automatically remove a finding. Where review identifies a material uncertainty, that uncertainty is shown beside the finding while it is under review.
If the evidence no longer supports a finding, the finding is WITHDRAWN, clearly marked as withdrawn, and no longer counted in the current grade. Any affected grade is recalculated.
Material corrections are appended to the record — never overwritten — and show what changed, why it changed, and when.
Records show their observation period, last update, and methodology version. Public availability depends on the service continuing to operate; we do not promise permanent hosting.
Our own calls are graded on The Board by the same ruler. Manual overrides, if ever made, are published on the record with their reason and effect. Placement is never sold without disclosure.
That includes the founder's key. Same rules, same endings, same drops. No exceptions at the top.
